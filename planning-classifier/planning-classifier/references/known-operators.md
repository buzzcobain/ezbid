# Known Operators — PBSA and Co-living

*Use this to identify whether an applicant or agent is a known sector operator, which affects
both classification confidence and the recommended approach for an interior design firm.*

---

**Procurement note for Flagstaffe:** Large PBSA operators often have preferred supplier lists
or framework agreements. The best approach is usually to contact the developer (not the
operator) at pre-application or early submission stage, before procurement is locked.
Flagstaffe's early-involvement proposition — budget stress-testing, specification input,
programme planning — is most valuable before the main contractor is appointed. Smaller
or one-off developers are more open to direct introductions and benefit most from
Flagstaffe's guidance on FF&E budgets and specification.

**Procurement note:** Co-living operators invest heavily in communal spaces as a commercial
product — the FF&E in lobbies, lounges, co-working areas, and gyms directly drives occupancy
and brand perception. Flagstaffe's bespoke manufacturing capability and commercial-grade
specification knowledge are well-suited to these spaces. Approach at planning submission or
shortly after, before the main contractor's supply chain is locked in.

## Flags for unknown applicants

If the applicant name is:
- A generic SPV / property company name (e.g. "XYZ Developments Ltd", "123 Property Holdings")
  → probably a one-off developer. More likely to be open to introductions. Less likely to have
  an established supply chain. Worth approaching.
- A large housebuilder (Barratt, Taylor Wimpey, Berkeley) → likely C3 BTR, less relevant.
- A university itself → PBSA, likely has internal procurement. Worth monitoring.
- A known operator from the lists above → note the operator and their procurement style above.
