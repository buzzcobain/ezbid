# Bid Template — Writing Guidance for Flagstaffe

Flagstaffe is an FF&E supply and installation specialist, not an interior design practice.
All language must reflect that. The value proposition is: programme certainty, budget
reliability, specification expertise, bespoke manufacturing, and quality installation.

---

## Tone of voice

Flagstaffe's voice is grounded, expert, and direct. They have done this at serious scale
and they know it. The tone should be:

- **Confident without being flashy** — the track record speaks. State facts, not superlatives.
- **Programme-focused** — developers and contractors care most about the end date and the
  budget. Lead with reliability.
- **Practically expert** — Dan and Kam know more about FF&E specification than most of their
  clients. Let that show without being condescending.
- **Straightforward** — no marketing fluff. Flagstaffe's blog is a good model: plain English,
  useful, no waffle.

Avoid: "passionate about", "excited to", "world-class", "unique approach".
These are filler. Flagstaffe earns credibility with specifics.

---

## Cover letter

**Purpose:** Establish relevance. Show you've read the application. Request a conversation.

**Length:** 1 page. 4–5 paragraphs.

**Structure:**
1. Why Flagstaffe is writing — reference the specific scheme and planning stage
2. Why Flagstaffe is well placed — 2–3 specific facts that match this project
3. What early engagement means for the applicant — budget, programme, spec
4. Call to action — a specific, low-friction ask (call, introductory meeting)
5. Sign-off from Dan / Kam

**Example opening (PBSA, post-submission):**
"We came across [APPLICATION REFERENCE] on the [LPA] planning portal — [ADDRESS] — and
wanted to get in touch at this stage. With consent likely in the coming months, the time
to be thinking about FF&E procurement is now, not after you've broken ground."

**Example opening (PBSA, pre-application):**
"We work with developers and contractors at pre-application stage on exactly this kind of
scheme. Getting FF&E right from the start — budget, spec, programme — saves significantly
more than it costs. We'd like to talk about [ADDRESS] before the brief is locked."

**Example opening (co-living):**
"Co-living schemes at [LOCATION] tend to live or die on the quality of their communal
spaces. That's the FF&E. We've delivered communal FF&E packages for [OPERATOR TYPE]
schemes at this scale and we'd welcome the chance to talk about [ADDRESS]."

## Proposed approach

**Purpose:** Show Flagstaffe understands this project's specific pressures.

**For PBSA schemes:**

The three things every PBSA developer or contractor worries about:
1. Programme — will it be ready for the start of term?
2. Budget — is the cost-per-bed-space going to hold?
3. Specification — will it match the design intent and withstand student use?

Frame Flagstaffe's approach around these three.

Early involvement talking points:
- "We can stress-test your budget expectations at specification stage — before anything
  is committed"
- "Our bespoke manufacturing capability means the spec doesn't have to be compromised
  to hit cost targets"
- "We only take on a managed number of projects, so your programme gets dedicated resource"

**For co-living / BTR schemes:**

Communal spaces are the product for co-living operators. The FF&E in those spaces
directly affects occupancy, brand perception, and resident retention.

Talking points:
- "Communal FF&E needs to be specified for commercial use, not residential — durability,
  cleanability, replaceability all matter differently"
- "We've supplied and installed [COMMUNAL SPACE TYPES] on schemes of this type"
- "Getting the FF&E specification right for communal spaces early avoids costly
  value-engineering later"

**Programme section:**
Reference the application's current stage. Typical timelines:

| Stage | Flagstaffe's involvement | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-application / pre-submission | Budget planning, early specification input, design coordination | Ongoing |
| Planning determination | Procurement preparation, specification lock, supply chain mobilisation | 8–16 weeks |
| Post-consent | Full FF&E procurement, manufacturing, delivery scheduling | 12–20 weeks |
| On site | Installation by CSCS crews, quality assurance, snagging | Programme-dependent |
| Handover | Final QA, defects resolution, handover pack | 2–4 weeks |

## Why Flagstaffe for this scheme

**Purpose:** The sharpest section. One focused half-page connecting Flagstaffe's strengths
to the specific application.

Write this last. Look at the classifier output and the scheme details. Pick 2–3 things
about this scheme that play directly to Flagstaffe's strengths. Write to those specifically.

**For a large scheme (200+ beds):**
"A scheme at this scale is where our experience shows most clearly. We've delivered
FF&E packages for projects over [X] rooms — we know the procurement complexity, the
manufacturing lead times, and the installation sequencing that a programme like this
demands. We manage it so you don't have to."

**For a scheme with complex communal spaces:**
"The amenity programme here — [LIST FROM APPLICATION] — represents significant FF&E
scope. These aren't bedroom packages. They need specification that holds up commercially,
sourcing that can deliver at the quantities required, and installation crews that understand
the difference. That's what we do."

**For a first-time developer / SPV:**
"If this is your first PBSA scheme, the FF&E element can feel opaque — costs vary widely,
lead times bite if you're late engaging, and specification errors are expensive to fix.
Getting Flagstaffe in early removes that uncertainty. We've done this at scale, we know
where the risks are, and we can de-risk them for you."

---

## What NOT to include

- Do not promise concept design, mood boards, or interior design services
- Do not use the phrase "design-led" to describe Flagstaffe
- Do not imply Flagstaffe replaces the architect or interior designer
- Do not make claims about rates or cost-per-bed without checking firm-profile.md first
